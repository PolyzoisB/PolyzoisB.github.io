Research

Statistical models for complex seismicity.

My work combines stochastic point processes, Bayesian and simulation-based inference, clustering methods and machine learning to study earthquake occurrence and improve probabilistic interpretation and forecasting.

Simulation-based inference for ETAS

ETAS models are widely used to represent earthquake triggering, but inference can become challenging when catalogs are incomplete or the likelihood is computationally expensive. My recent work develops a likelihood-free framework that estimates ETAS-type models through simulation-based inference, combining stochastic simulation, summary statistics and Bayesian calibration.

The broader aim is to make inference more robust under realistic observation conditions and to support short-term probabilistic forecasting when standard maximum-likelihood assumptions are strained.

Methods
ETAS / ETASI
Approximate Bayesian computation
Sequential Monte Carlo
Stochastic optimisation
Catalog incompleteness

Earthquake clustering and declustering

A second line of research concerns the separation of background seismicity from triggered activity. I work with nearest-neighbour representations of earthquake catalogs and automated procedures for identifying statistically meaningful clustering scales.

This includes the Susceptibility Index approach and automatic nearest-neighbour declustering, designed to reduce dependence on manually selected thresholds and to provide an interpretable, non-parametric partition of seismicity.

Methods
Nearest-neighbour distance
Susceptibility Index
Non-parametric declustering
Synthetic ETAS validation
Cluster diagnostics

Triggering mechanisms and repeating earthquakes

I study clustered earthquake sequences through complementary statistical and waveform-based approaches. In the 2020–2021 Gulf of Corinth seismic crisis, this included the identification of repeating-earthquake families, analysis of migration patterns and integration with triggering interpretations based on stress transfer, fluid processes and aseismic deformation.

The objective is not only to classify events statistically, but also to connect observed spatio-temporal structure with plausible physical mechanisms.

Methods
Waveform cross-correlation
DBSCAN
Repeating earthquakes
Migration analysis
Triggering interpretation

Non-stationary stochastic modelling

Earlier work used Markovian Arrival Processes to represent changes between low- and high-activity seismic regimes and to describe long-term variability in earthquake occurrence. These models were applied to clustering analysis and pseudo-prospective forecasting of large earthquakes.

Methods
Markovian Arrival Processes
Hidden-state modelling
AIC / BIC
Local decoding
Pseudo-prospective testing

Machine learning for anomaly detection

During my work in cybersecurity research, I developed machine-learning workflows for network anomaly and novelty detection. These included autoencoders, one-class support vector machines, federated-learning experiments and methods for improving model generalisation across heterogeneous data environments.

Autoencoders
One-class SVM
Novelty detection
Federated learning
Cross-domain generalisation
